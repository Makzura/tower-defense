# Calibration — scored once, before any change

Scored from the captured observations in `captures/`, against
`DIRECTION.md` (criterion 7) and `PERF.md` (criterion 8).

**45 groups scored. 0 pass the bar.**

A score is never revised downward for an unchanged asset. It changes only
when the asset changes.

## The two anchors

=== 1. THE WORST ASSET: G13, the Siphon (5/40) ===

What is actually on screen in 13-siphon-close-before.png and 12-sniper-a5-close-before.png: three stacked cylinders — a squat pad, a body, a flat cap — in one flat lavender-grey, sitting to the right of the Warbringer and the Sniper A5. No panel line, no seam, no emissive, no aperture, no person, no hardware. gl-world.js:256-272 confirms it is exactly that and nothing more: `towerCylinder()` builds pad/body/cap from GLGeometry.cylinder in the tower tint at 0.35 value for the pad and cap, cached once. There is no js/gl/models/siphon-*.js file at all; the models directory has 24 meshes and none of them is a Siphon.

Three things make this the floor rather than merely another empty slot:

(a) It INVERTS the value ladder instead of just failing to honour it. DIRECTION rule 3 says the largest surface takes the darkest value. The Siphon's lavender-grey is the LIGHTEST object anywhere on the board — lighter than the road, lighter than every authored model, lighter than the slab. In 11-towers-maxtier-before.png, at the default play camera, all seven max-tier towers collapse into a ~30px black smudge and the ONLY object you can identify is the placeholder cylinder, because it is the only thing bright enough to read. The stand-in out-shouts every piece of authored work in the game. That is worse than invisible.

(b) Its colour is not in the shared palette at all. iron/iron2/steel/steel_dark/brass/walnut/leather/ember/ley/ley_dim contains nothing that lavender-grey belongs to. It is a tint fallback, not a choice.

(c) It is not cheap content. meta.js prices it at 150 meta coins — the most expensive unlock in the armoury, described in the source's own comment as "a longer project" — and $800 in a run. The player pays the game's highest unlock cost and receives a primitive. In 09-screen-armoury-before.png its store icon is a green ball on a stick.

SCORING THE FLOOR: silhouette 1 (a cylinder is a legible shape that names nothing), form 0, material 0 (flat lambert, no surface intent), colour 0 (wrong value, off-palette, actively harmful), motion 1 (gl-world.js:588 gives it a faint cooldown breathe and that is the entire animation budget), effect n/a, cohesion 0, perf 5. The 5 on perf is unearned in the sense that matters: it is cheap because it is empty.

WHY NOT THE TYRANT, AND WHY NOT G03. The Tyrant (G17, 9/40) is the worse FAILURE by consequence — it is the payoff of a 35-wave campaign and 03-enemies-spheres-before.png shows it as a single low-poly red sphere with a darker band cylinder ringing it, under a 5000/5000 HP boss bar and an announce line reading THE TYRANT ROARS. But it is the same code path as the other fifteen (gl-world.js:230, `enemySphere`), only bigger, and its red at least matches DIRECTION's fixed meaning for enemy cores. The Siphon gets less right per criterion. G03 map scenery (3/30) has the lowest average of all, but 00-board-default and all six 10-map captures confirm there are literally zero props — an absence cannot anchor a scale, because there is no execution to calibrate against. The floor has to be a thing you can look at.

=== 2. THE BEST ASSET: G26, the B5 channel + strike + ritual circle (28/40) ===

This is the only group in the table above 25 and the only one where the source reads like someone designed a moment rather than drew a shape. gl-world.js:1891-1939 and 2179-2223, plus the flat ritual circle visible on the board in 12-sniper-a5 and 14-warbringer-a5:

- Three seconds of ANTICIPATION before anything happens: three counter-rotating ritual rings (12 ticks clockwise, 6 anticlockwise at 1.6x, a 0-tick core at 2.2x) opening from 0.35 to 1.0 of the AoE radius while stroke width climbs 1.6 to 3.8 and alpha 0.30 to 0.90.
- The telegraph is a DIRECTION, not just a place: a light column projected from four world-space corners, growing on `420 * p * p` — quadratic, so it accelerates into the hit.
- COST IS DRAWN. A fourth ring closes INWARD on the caster (`ul(26) * (1.4 - 0.7*p)`, negative spin) while the target's rings open outward. The tower pays permanent max HP for the ability and you can see the man paying. That is narrative carried by motion, and nothing else in this build attempts it.
- FOLLOW-THROUGH with non-linear easing: on resolve the column slams to 520 on `1 - pow(age, 0.35)` and collapses, with a separate `snap = 1 - age*5` term pinching the width; the seal is thrown outward and fades on `fade*fade`; a scorch ring outlasts both. Anticipation, impact, decay, residue — the full envelope, on curves, none of them linear.
- Cohesion is clean rather than merely inoffensive: SHOT_RGB.sigil is the violet DIRECTION reserves for the Sniper's ritual and covenant, and DIRECTION explicitly permits canvas for "a rune circle in mid-air", which is precisely what this is. It is the one effect in the game that is on-canvas for the right reason.

Where it still loses: material 2 (a stroked line has no surface — it is light, not matter), form 3 (the seal geometry is generic ticks-on-a-circle, not an authored sigil that means anything), effect 3 (it lands but there is no world reaction — no shake, no debris, no lit ground).

=== 3. WHAT THE GAP MEANS FOR THE SCALE ===

Floor 5/40, ceiling 28/40, on a 40-point scale where the passing bar is 32 (4 on every criterion). The single most important calibration fact: THE BEST ASSET IN THE PROJECT STILL FAILS. 28/40 averages 3.5. That is correct and it should stay that way — nothing here has been scored to a shipping bar, and the scale is not being asked to flatter the build.

The rungs, concretely, in this project's own terms:

0 — the thing does not exist and nothing stands in for it. The fissures in G24 (nothing in 15/16-effects-aoe-quake), the screen transitions in G45 (grep confirms ZERO `@keyframes` and ZERO CSS `transition` rules anywhere in the build — every screen change is a hard cut), the props in G03.

1 — A GEOMETRIC PRIMITIVE OR A BROWSER DEFAULT OCCUPYING THE SLOT. The Siphon's stacked cylinders. The Tyrant's sphere. `600 14px system-ui` drifting upward at `y -= 26*dt` — constant velocity, no ease, no scale — which is what every cash popup in the game is. It says "something is here" and stops.

2 — AUTHORED BUT GENERIC. Correct volumes, correct colour family, no material intent, no easing, legible only at close range. Most enemy and tower meshes live here: 02-enemies-modelled shows a Brute and two Normals with real machine silhouettes and red cores, and 00-board-default shows the same Brute as an indistinct dark lump.

3 — AUTHORED, CORRECT AND LEGIBLE, WITH ONE AXIS CLEARLY MISSING. The Rifleman base in 01-towers-base: real top hat, real long coat, real drum rifle — and value-flat against the board so the costume dies at play distance. The Warbringer A5 in 14-warbringer-a5: real smith, real hammer, leather reading as leather — and not one trace of ley in the metal, which is exactly what DIRECTION says path A is for.

4 — THE SHIPPING BAR, and here it means four things at once: it reads in one glance at the DEFAULT camera (not the close-up capture), its light comes from geometry via uGlow rather than a sprite laid over it, its motion has anticipation and follow-through on a curve, and a stranger could name what it is and what it means without being told. Only G26 clears that on more than two criteria.

5 — nothing in this build. Nothing should be scored 5 until something first clears 4 on all eight.

One more calibration note that cuts across the whole table: PERF IS THE ONLY CRITERION WHERE THIS PROJECT SCORES WELL, AND IT SCORES WELL FOR THE WRONG REASON. PERF.md measures stress at 2.89 ms GPU-fenced against a 6.0 ms ceiling at 720p, and says outright that triangle count is NOT the sensitive axis (141,288 tris across all 25 models). There is roughly 3 ms of unspent budget. Every high perf score in this table should be read as "budget sitting idle", not "well optimised". The pass should spend it on geometry and emissive materials — the axis that is cheap — and must NOT spend it on full-screen passes or large translucent overlays, which are the one thing that scales 3.61x at 4K and breaks the ceiling.

TOO HARSH — nudge these up:

* G04 board void / backdrop, 12/30 -> 15/30, and cohesion should NOT be its weakest. Its failing is emptiness (no ley bleed, no horizon, no depth cue), which is form and effect, not cohesion. Scoring it as incoherent penalises the one correct value decision on the board.

ENDORSED AS CORRECTLY PLACED, for reference: G14 recruit summons at 25/40 is the right runner-up — gl-world.js:274-289 gives the recruits a two-phase recoil off the shot's own cooldown clock (35% KICK, remainder SETTLE with a deliberate overshoot) on real rig groups, which is genuine follow-through on authored geometry. G20 covenant round at 23/40 and G26 at 28 are the only other groups that show design intent rather than shape production. STRUCTURAL WARNING ABOUT THE MIDDLE OF THE TABLE: 21 of 48 groups sit between 14 and 21. That band is currently doing double duty for "authored but generic" (rung 2) and "authored and nearly there" (rung 3), and the two are not distinguishable by their totals. Every drift flag above pushes downward out of that band, deliberately — the band needs to be pulled apart before any post-pass re-score, or improvement will be invisible in the numbers.

### Attack order

Attack in this order. The first two are causes; almost everything else in the table is a symptom of them, and fixing symptoms first is work that gets redone.

1. G06 SHADER LIGHTING MODEL (12/30) — FIRST, ALONE, BEFORE ANY ART. gl-renderer.js:75-83 is two lambert terms plus a slate ambient (`ambient [0.125,0.142,0.180]`), with no specular, no roughness, no rim and no fresnel. `material` appears in the weakest list of roughly 26 of 48 groups and NOT ONE of them is fixable at the asset level while the shader has no material response to give. Add a spec/roughness term and a camera-facing rim/fresnel and every mesh in the game gains a lit edge against the dark board for the cost of one uniform. PERF.md gives you ~3 ms of headroom and says geometry is the cheap axis; a per-fragment rim term is exactly the right place to spend it.

2. G07 SHARED PALETTE + G01 SLAB VALUE + G05 GRID — the second systemic cause. 11-towers-maxtier is the proof: at the default camera all seven max-tier towers merge into one ~30px black smudge, and the only legible object is a placeholder cylinder. The board and the models are occupying the same narrow slice of luminance — the exact failure DIRECTION rule 3 names and says already happened once to the Rifleman. Separate slab from models by VALUE before touching a single mesh, or every silhouette fix downstream gets re-eaten by the background.

3. G13 SIPHON, then G17 TYRANT, then G16 THE SIXTEEN SPHERES — the three groups where the honest answer is "no asset exists". In that order and for these reasons: the Siphon because it is the game's most expensive unlock (150 meta coins) AND currently the brightest object on the board, so it inverts the value ladder and out-reads authored work; the Tyrant because it is the payoff of a 35-wave campaign and is presently a ball under a 5000 HP bar; the sixteen spheres last because they all run through one function (gl-world.js:230) and want a small shared kit of machine bodies keyed by type — plate, cage, housing, exhaust, a lit core through a slot — not sixteen bespoke models.

4. G09 GROUNDING AND CONTACT SHADOWS (9/40) — cheap, systemic, and it is the difference between a mesh sitting on a surface and hovering above it. One fix lifts every model group at once. Constraint from PERF.md: do this with geometry or small quads, never a full-screen soft-shadow pass — fill rate is the sensitive axis and 4K multiplies it 3.61x.

5. G45 TRANSITIONS + THE WHOLE HUD MOTION FAMILY (G33-G43). There are ZERO `@keyframes` and ZERO CSS `transition` declarations in the entire build; every screen change is a hard cut and nothing in the HUD eases. Define one motion vocabulary — a duration scale, two curves, a paired enter/exit — and apply it across all of them. This is the single cheapest lift on the `motion` criterion, which is weakest in ~21 groups.

6. G39 CASH POPUPS — small, fast, and currently a visible bug. Fix `+$undefined` (effects.js:349) before anything cosmetic, then give the popup a scale-pop on birth and an ease-out rise instead of `y -= 26*dt`, and take it off `system-ui`.

7. G42 INDEX + G43 ARMOURY — re-skin in the menu's own terminal language. 06-screen-menu proves that language already exists and is good (framing corners, ley core with concentric emissive rings, relay dish, hazard-stripe footer); 08 and 09 are unstyled dark rectangles that simply don't use it. While in there, fix the menu's own strings for cohesion: it reads TOWER DEFENSE where DIRECTION records LEYLINE DEFENCE NETWORK / COMMAND TERMINAL, and the subtitle claims "2 towers" when there are four.

EXPLICITLY DO NOT START WITH: G10 / G11 / G12, the individual tower tier meshes. They score 19-21 and their weakest criteria are silhouette and colour — both caused by items 1 and 2 above. Re-modelling any tower tier before the shader and the value ladder are fixed is work that will have to be thrown away.

## Per-group detail

### `G03` map scenery props

- **scores:** silhouette 0, form 0, material 0, colour 0, motion n/a, effect n/a, cohesion 0, perf 3
- **weakest:** silhouette, form, material, colour, cohesion
- **captures used:** 00-board-default-before.png, 07-screen-chooser-before.png, 10-map-rune-circuit-before.png, 10-map-mana-coil-before.png, 10-map-sigil-lattice-before.png, 10-map-null-meridian-before.png, 10-map-shifting-ley-before.png, 10-map-twin-confluence-before.png

CONFIRMED ABSENT. buildMapMesh reads `Maps.ENVIRONMENTS[map.id]` and then iterates `env.zones` only — `env.models` is never touched, and grep finds no other reference to models, decorations, roadEdge, roadCenter, accent2 or panelLine anywhere under js/gl/. game.js calls Maps.drawEnvironment (the function that draws the props) only inside the `if (!world3D)` branch at line 2814, so the 3D path skips it entirely.

The dead data is substantial: 9 authored props per map x 6 maps = 54 machines (antenna, server, reactor, console, pylon, tank, vent, holo, battery, coil, gate) plus each map's `decorations` list plus the accent "circuit trunk" lines that connect the machines in pairs. All of it ships and none of it draws.

The captures agree. Capture 00 and all six 10-map-* captures show a floor, some slabs, a road, and nothing else standing on the board — the only non-actor object anywhere is the grey placeholder Siphon cylinder at (848,382). The contrast is stated most sharply by capture 07: the chooser cards render the full 2D environment — reactors, consoles, holos, hazard hatching, glowing accent trunks — so the game shows the player a facility on the card it sells them and then hands them an empty floor.

This is the largest single cohesion hole in the board. DIRECTION's setting is a leyline defence network on "a worked surface with power running through it", and props are the entire mechanism by which a surface reads as worked. Without them the board passes quick test 1 only by being flat and dark.

Silhouette, form, material, colour and cohesion score 0 rather than null: these assets are inventoried, they exist in data, and their observable contribution to every frame is nothing. Motion and effect are genuinely null — even the 2D versions are static line art with no animation and no effect layer.

Perf 3, and the score is a criticism rather than a credit. Nothing is spent, but PERF.md states plainly that triangle count is not the sensitive axis (all 25 models together are 141 288 triangles) and that the derived worst case still leaves ~6 ms of headroom. Static, non-instanced, non-translucent prop meshes baked into the existing map mesh are the cheapest quality available anywhere in this project, and none of it is being bought. That is a proportionality failure in the direction of underspend, not a budget decision.

**First change to make:** Extrude the eleven prop kinds as small procedural meshes in GLGeometry and bake them into the map mesh in buildMapMesh from env.models, using the same authored x/y/size/rotation and the ul(x / AUTHORED_PX_PER_UL) conversion the zones already use. They go in the existing single map draw call at zero per-frame cost, and the reactor/coil/pylon kinds are where the first emissive ley geometry on the board belongs.

### `G13` The Siphon (placeholder cylinder, no model)

- **scores:** silhouette 0, form 0, material 0, colour 1, motion 1, effect 0, cohesion 0, perf 3
- **weakest:** silhouette, form, material, effect, cohesion
- **captures used:** 13-siphon-close-before.png, 11-towers-maxtier-before.png, 09-screen-armoury-before.png

Confirmed in 13-siphon-close (magnified) and 11-towers-maxtier, and in source: there is no siphon mesh anywhere — no siphon-*.js in js/gl/models and no tower_siphon.py in tools/blender. towerModel() returns null and the tower falls through to towerCylinder() at js/gl/gl-world.js:255-272, which builds exactly three primitives: a 14-sided pad, a 12-sided body and a 12-sided cap, roughly 100 triangles, tinted from tower.tint(). **First change to make:** Ship one projected blob quad per actor on the ground plane, multiplicative rather than a canvas ellipse so it darkens the deck instead of painting over it. ### `G08` emission / uGlow channel

- **scores:** silhouette 2, form 1, material 1, colour 1, motion 2, effect 2, cohesion 1, perf 3
- **weakest:** colour, form, material, cohesion
- **captures used:** 01-towers-base-before.png, 02-enemies-modelled-before.png, 04-combat-wide-before.png, 05-combat-close-before.png, 11-towers-maxtier-before.png, 12-sniper-a5-close-before.png, 13-siphon-close-before.png, 14-warbringer-a5-close-before.png

The architecture is right and the delivery is not. And setGlow is called at exactly two sites in the codebase, both inside the tower loop in gl-world.js, reset to 0 immediately after, so EVERY enemy in the game draws at uGlow 0: the README's defining enemy feature, a red core behind cage bars, is never driven at all despite four enemy models carrying core materials authored up to emission 4.2. None of it breaches a PERF.md ceiling, but the ratio is bad.

**First change to make:** Carry emission COLOUR per vertex instead of per draw, and delete the baked white floor. The palette already stores it as [r,g,b,emission] and expand() already reads it — add an aEmiCol attribute (or fold it into the existing colour path), stop adding min(1,emit*0.16) into the base colour, and make the term lit += vEmiCol * vEmi * (uRestGlow + uGlow). ### `G06` shader lighting model

The whole model is nine lines: lit = vCol * (uAmbient + max(dot(n,keyDir),0)*1.02 + uFillColor*max(dot(n,fillDir),0)), plus additive emission, plus a height lift, plus one pow(1/2.2). The Rifleman's own body occupies 0.071-0.184 with only 236 of 7881 pixels above 0.35 lum; his brightest surface is 0.013 above the floor he stands on. There is no rim, no fresnel, no wrap, no edge term of any kind, and the vertex shader never passes a world position or view direction so none is possible. The key sits at 68 degrees elevation, inherited from the 2D shadow-offset maths in td_scene.py, so on a standing figure — almost entirely vertical surface — the sun contributes at most cos(68) = 0.37 of its strength while horizontal surfaces get 0.93. FORM (2): flat shading does give real facets and the enemy plating in 02 shows honest top/side breaks, but with a near-overhead key, a fill at one sixth the key's strength and no rim, character forms collapse into one mass. COLOUR (2): the linear-light/sRGB-out transfer is genuinely right and GLGeometry.hex, GLModels.toLinear and td_scene.srgb do use the same exact curve, so the road and the man on it agree about a hex value. The risk, and why colour is a 3 rather than a 4, is that coat/coat_dark/canvas/rust/leather with no emissive is a narrow dark-earth band on the same dark-teal board that already flattened the Rifleman, and this project's recurring failure is exactly the value ladder. SILHOUETTE follows from that: humanoids at enemy scale, in five dark colours, walking among enemies whose meshes are 3.9k–7.8k triangles, with no bright note to break them. MATERIAL: summon_recruit.py authors roughness and metallic per material (gun 0.40/0.45 metallic, leather 0.80, skin 0.70) and none of it survives export — the runtime palette is [r,g,b,emissive] and the shader is Lambert with no specular — so a gun-blue carbine and a wool coat respond to light identically. EFFECT: no emissive by design, so nothing for uGlow to drive; what they do carry is right — recruitAim() reads the shipped gun group's own breech and muzzle extremes to derive the barrel-levelling pitch and the offset that lands the tip exactly on SoldierRecruit.fire's spawn point, so rounds leave the actual barrel rather than a point near it. PERF: ~5.4k triangles each, six draw ranges, 8 frames, up to two per B4 tower and four per B5, aim cached once per model. All geometry, no fill rate — the axis PERF explicitly calls insensitive. Comfortably inside all three ceilings.

**First change to make:** Capture them before doing anything else — this group was scored without a single render of it, and silhouette, material and colour are all judged from palette data rather than pixels. Then the change the palette already predicts: give each recruit one light-value surface (the B5 helmet, the B4 face-rag) so a five-colour dark-earth figure is not a black smear on a dark-teal board.
